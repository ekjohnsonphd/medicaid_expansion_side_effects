# One-minute poster pitch — WIC, 9 September 2026

The session coordinator will cut you off at 60 seconds. The instructions ask for
**title, research question, major finding** — nothing else. This is ~140 words,
which is about 55 seconds at a measured pace.

---

> **Who Pays After Medicaid Expansion? Cross-payer effects on health spending in
> US states.**
>
> We know Medicaid expansion raised Medicaid enrollment and Medicaid spending.
> What we don't know is what it did to everyone else — whether that spending
> displaced private insurance, or Medicare, or what households paid out of pocket.
>
> So we asked: did expansion shift the burden between payers, or add to it?
>
> Using state-level spending by payer from 2010 to 2018, comparing the 25 states
> that expanded in January 2014 against the 19 that never did, we find the first
> stage you'd expect — Medicaid coverage up nearly four points, private coverage
> down two.
>
> But spending by other payers doesn't move. Not Medicare, not private, not
> out-of-pocket, in any care setting.
>
> And we can say how hard we looked: spillovers above three percent for Medicare
> and seven percent for private are ruled out. That's the finding — the money
> didn't move between payers.

---

## Delivery notes

- The one sentence that has to land is the last one. If you're running out of
  time, cut the coverage numbers, not the bound.
- Don't say "null result" out loud — say what is ruled out. It's the same claim
  and it sounds like a finding rather than an absence.
- Expect the question: *"isn't out-of-pocket the interesting one?"* The honest
  answer is on the poster — that interval spans a 22% fall to an 11% rise, so
  this design can't speak to it. Say so plainly; it's a limitation, not a hole.
- Second likely question: *"why only 2014 expanders?"* Because the later cohorts
  are 3, 3 and 1 states, and at that size the estimator's variance isn't
  identified — a one-state cohort produces a falsely significant effect about
  40% of the time in placebo runs.
- Practise it aloud against a timer. The instructions specifically ask you to.
