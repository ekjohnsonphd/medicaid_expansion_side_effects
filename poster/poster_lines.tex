\begin{minipage}[t][\colh][t]{\colw}
\panel{The question}{
  \Body{Medicaid expansion raised Medicaid enrollment and Medicaid spending. What it did to
  \emph{everyone else} is less settled --- whether the new coverage displaced private
  insurance, and whether the new spending displaced anyone else's.}
  \vspace{4mm}
  \Body{We follow coverage, spending and utilisation for every major payer through the
  same design, so the answers have to add up.}
}
\vfill
\panel{Data and design}{
  \Body{IHME Disease Expenditure Project (DEX 2.0): state-level spending and encounters by
  payer and setting, 2010--2018. Uninsurance from CPS.}
  \vspace{4mm}
  \Body{Difference-in-differences comparing the \Num{25 states that expanded on 1 January
  2014} with the \Num{19 that never expanded}. Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) group-time
  estimator, never-treated controls, adjusting for poverty, median income, pre-expansion
  uninsurance and age structure. Standard errors clustered by state, multiplier bootstrap.}
  \vspace{4mm}
  \Note{Later cohorts are excluded: they contain 3, 3 and 1 states, and at that size a
  placebo test rejects for a one-state cohort 88\% of the time.}
  \vspace{4mm}
  \Body{Enrollee counts and population are recovered from the reported ratios, so coverage
  is measured inside the same source as spending. On the all-ages basis all four payers
  return the same population to within \Num{0.005\%}.}
}
\vfill
\panel{Spending has three parts}{
  \Body{\[\frac{\text{spend}}{\text{resident}}=\frac{\text{spend}}{\text{encounter}}\times
  \frac{\text{encounters}}{\text{enrollee}}\times\frac{\text{enrollees}}{\text{resident}}\]}
  \vspace{2mm}
  \Body{Price, use, and coverage. An identity, so each margin is estimated separately and
  read against the others.}
}
\vfill
\panel{Limitations}{
  \Body{Estimates describe 2014 expanders only.}
  \vspace{3mm}
  \Body{\textbf{Out-of-pocket spending is not analysed.} DEX defines it as insured
  cost-sharing plus all spending by the uninsured, and expansion changes that mix directly.
  Its pre-trend test fails in every setting.}
  \vspace{3mm}
  \Body{Private coverage changes cannot be attributed to individual switching: this design
  identifies group differences, not flows.}
}
\end{minipage}

\panel{1. Expansion covered the uninsured}{
  \includegraphics[width=\colw]{figures/f1_coverage.pdf}
  \vspace{3mm}
  \Note{Blue: the 25 states that expanded in 2014. Red: the 19 that never expanded.}
  \vspace{5mm}
  {\fontsize{27}{38}\selectfont\input{tables/t1_coverage.tex}\par}
  \vspace{4mm}
  \Body{Medicaid rose \Num{3.7 points}. The uninsured rate fell \Num{1.4 points} further
  than in control states.}
  \vspace{3mm}
  \Body{\textbf{Private coverage rose in both groups} --- 62.9 to 64.4\% in expanders,
  62.6 to 66.5\% in controls. The negative estimate is control states gaining faster, not
  expansion states losing. Marketplace subsidies cover 100--138\% FPL where Medicaid does
  not.}
}
\vfill
\hpanel{2. Spending followed enrollment, not intensity}{
  \includegraphics[width=\hpw]{figures/f2_medicaid_lines.pdf}
  \vspace{2mm}
  \Note{Blue: the 25 states that expanded in 2014. Red: the 19 that never expanded.}
  \vspace{4mm}
  \Body{Medicaid spending per resident rose \Num{18.0\%} (SE 4.0). Per enrollee it did not
  move: \Num{$-3.3\%$} (SE 4.2).}
  \vspace{3mm}
  \Body{Solving the identity, a new enrollee costs about \Num{80\%} of an incumbent
  (95\% CI 0.29 to 1.31). The expansion population was slightly cheaper than the existing
  caseload, not dramatically so.}
}
\end{minipage}

\begin{minipage}[t][\colh][t]{\colw}
\panel{3. Weak signs of a changed private pool}{
  \includegraphics[width=\colw]{figures/f3_private_lines.pdf}
  \vspace{3mm}
  \Body{Private spending per enrollee \Num{$+4.8\%$} (SE 2.8) and encounters per enrollee
  \Num{$+3.3\%$} (SE 2.4). Neither reaches significance.}
  \vspace{3mm}
  \Body{Because both margins move together, this is \textbf{use, not price}. The most
  likely reading is dilution of the \emph{control} group's risk pool as lower-utilising
  marketplace enrollees joined it.}
}
\vfill
\panel{4. No spillover to other payers}{
  \includegraphics[width=\colw]{figures/f4_payers_lines.pdf}
  \vspace{3mm}
  \Body{Medicare spending per resident \Num{$+0.4\%$} (SE 1.3) --- nothing, on any margin.
  Private spending per resident \Num{$+1.7\%$} (SE 2.5).}
  \vspace{4mm}
  \Body{Across \textbf{84 estimates} covering four payers, five settings and four margins,
  every result significant at 5\% is a Medicaid result. Among the 18 non-Medicaid spending
  models, \Num{none} is significant --- chance alone would give about one.}
}
